# 0003 — a digest cut at its budget

**Claim.** The digest puts findings first, so a digest cut at its budget still carries the shortlist and its **Why:** lines, and the briefing matches the uncut one.

**Input.** 0001's repo, `bash fixtures.sh 0001 <dir>`, with `DOC_SCOUT_BUDGET=2500` in the arm's environment. The real corpora that overflowed before this version ran 30 to 55 KB; a 2,500-byte budget on this corpus reproduces the cut without one.

**Prompt.** `/eval:brief Add per-user caching to GET /lists so repeat loads are fast.`

**Result.** Batch 6:

- One scout call, unpiped. The digest, 2.9 KB with its cut line, ends:

  ```
  == DIGEST CUT at 2500 of 7629 bytes, inside section 3 (INDEX ENTRIES); not shown: 4 (ROOT INSTRUCTION FILES), 5 (INVENTORY). Read them in <repo>/.git/doc-scout-report.txt ==
  ```

- The shortlist and the headings with **Why:** lines survived the cut; the arm read the same five shortlisted docs as 0001, with `Read`.
- The same `docs/` rules as 0001's uncut run, each on its line, both rules of the deletion-window tension among them, plus `caching.md:11`, the link to how deletion reaches cached copies; `git status` clean.
- The full report opened, the first run of this case to do so, after the shortlisted docs. Its summary notes the two left-out sections and that `CLAUDE.md`, one of them, was already in context.
- 13 turns, $0.20.

The batch 2, 3, and 4 runs of this case left the report closed with no loss, which is why Step 1 says to open it when the shortlist misses a doc the task needs, not every time the digest is cut (see the README's History). Batch 6 opened it anyway; the briefing came out the same either way.
